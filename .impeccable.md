## Design Context

### Users
This portfolio is primarily for recruiters, engineering managers, and design-minded product teams evaluating Rimil Dey for full-time senior frontend and product engineering roles. A secondary audience is consulting and freelance prospects who need fast proof of credibility, communication quality, and product taste. Most visitors will scan quickly on laptop or mobile first, then decide whether to read deeper or reach out.

### Brand Personality
Editorial, modern, calm, sophisticated, and approachable. The site should feel composed and premium without becoming cold or self-important. It should signal strong frontend craft, product judgment, and clarity under constraint.

### Aesthetic Direction
Bias toward editorial / premium design inspired by the pacing and polish of fashion and publication sites like Vogue and Shopify Editions, but translated into a portfolio for a senior frontend engineer. The experience should privilege typography, rhythm, white space, and restrained visual contrast over gimmicks. Light theme should lead, with dark mode available but not dominant. Avoid generic developer-portfolio tropes: dense skill-chip walls, loud gradients, dashboard-style stat blocks, and overly playful motion.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with credibility fast: role, specialty, and current relevance should be understood within the first screen.
2. Favor editorial hierarchy over template blocks: use strong type, disciplined spacing, and narrative sequencing.
3. Show product-minded engineering, not just tool familiarity: emphasize impact, systems thinking, collaboration, and measurable outcomes.
4. Keep interaction quiet and confident: minimal JS, subtle motion, strong accessibility, and responsive layouts that feel intentionally composed at every breakpoint.
5. Make hiring action obvious: resume, contact, LinkedIn, GitHub, and project/work proof should always be easy to find.
